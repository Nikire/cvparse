# cvparse evaluation dataset (v1.0.0)

60 synthetic Spanish-language CVs with hard layouts, each paired with a hand-checkable
ground truth, used to benchmark cvparse against other resume parsers. **Every person, company, school,
phone number and e-mail address in this dataset is fictional**, generated by a seeded program
(`eval/generate/`). E-mails use only the reserved `example.com` / `example.org` domains. Any
resemblance to real people or organizations is coincidental.

License: the dataset (everything under `eval/dataset/`) is released under
[CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The generator code is MIT, like the rest of
the repository.

## Purpose

Rule-based and LLM resume parsers are usually tested on English, single-column CVs. This dataset
targets what breaks them in Spanish-speaking markets: two-column and sidebar PDFs, Word/Canva-style
table layouts and text boxes, functional and Europass-like CVs, scans, Spanish month names and
date ranges ("marzo 2021 – actualidad", "2019-21", "2018 a la fecha"), and country-specific
degrees (Licenciatura, Grado, Tecnicatura, Técnico Superior, Tecnólogo, Magíster, Diplomado...).

## Contents

```
manifest.json        DatasetEntry[] sorted by id (contract: eval/types.ts)
truth/<id>.json      ground truth for each CV (GroundTruth: a subset of the cvparse Resume schema)
files/<id>.<ext>     the rendered CV (.pdf, .docx or .png)
VERSION              dataset version (semver)
```

Ids look like `es-ar-001`: language prefix, country, per-country counter.

## How the ground truth maps to fields

The truth is a JSON Resume subset (`basics`, `work`, `education`, `skills`, `languages`)
plus `x_cvparse.educationLevels` and `x_cvparse.location`. Every truth value is drawn verbatim in
the file (only formatting is added), so a perfect parser can reach F1 = 1.0.

- `basics.name`, `basics.email`, `basics.phone`: exactly as printed (phone with its
  country-format spacing, e.g. `+54 9 11 4567-1234`, `+56 9 8765 4321`). Prefixes such as
  "Tel.:" or "Email:" are not part of the value. `basics.label` and `basics.summary` are included
  when printed (scanned CVs have no summary).
- `basics.location`: `{ city, region, countryCode }`. The printed text (`x_cvparse.location.raw`)
  is "City, Region, Country", or "City, Country" when city and region coincide (Lima, Montevideo).
- `work[]`: most recent first; `name` (company, including its legal suffix such as "S.A.S."),
  `position`, `location`, `startDate`, `endDate`, `highlights` (bullets; omitted in functional
  CVs, whose achievements are grouped by competency instead).
- Dates are ISO at the precision printed: `YYYY-MM` for month styles ("mar. 2021", "03/2021"),
  `YYYY` for year styles ("2019-21", "2019 – presente"). Ongoing roles have `endDate: null`.
- `education[]`: most recent first; `studyType` is the title family as printed ("Licenciatura",
  "Técnico Superior Universitario", "Magíster"), `area` the field ("Licenciatura en *Psicología*"),
  `institution`. Short courses carry a single date, stored as `endDate` with no `startDate`.
- `x_cvparse.educationLevels[i]`: `{ level, original, canonical }` for `education[i]`, using the
  cvparse enum (technical, bachelor, postgraduate, master, doctorate, course).
- `skills[]`: one `{ name }` per printed skill, no grouping (scorers should flatten any
  `keywords` in predictions).
- `languages[]`: `{ language, fluency }` with fluency exactly as printed ("Nativo", "C1",
  "Avanzado (C1)", "Intermedio").

Content that is printed but **not** in the truth: publications (academic CVs), competency headings
and achievements (functional CVs), the "CURRICULUM VITAE" banner and scanner stamps.

## Regenerating

```sh
npm run eval:dataset
```

The generator is seeded per CV id (mulberry32), pins PDF metadata dates and DOCX zip timestamps, and
writes keys in sorted order, so a second run is byte-identical. PNG/JPEG scans depend on the fonts
installed on the machine (Arial on Windows); regenerated scans can differ in bytes (not in content)
elsewhere, so the committed files are the reference. Bump `DATASET_VERSION` in
`eval/generate/plan.ts` whenever generated files or truth change.

## Who made this and known biases

The dataset, its generator, the ground truth and the scorer were all written by the cvparse
authors, the same people who wrote the parser being benchmarked. Treat the results as an
in-distribution benchmark, not an independent evaluation:

- **Date styles are in-distribution.** The date forms in the CVs ("marzo 2021", "mar. 2021",
  "03/2021", "2019-21", "2018 a la fecha", "verano 2020", ...) are the ones cvparse's date
  normalizer (`src/normalize/dates.ts`) was written to handle. Real CVs contain forms that neither
  the dataset nor the normalizer anticipates.
- **Degree vocabulary is in-distribution.** The degree titles (Licenciatura, Grado, Tecnicatura,
  Técnico Superior, Tecnólogo, Magíster, Diplomado, ...) were chosen alongside cvparse's degree
  normalizer (`src/normalize/education.ts`), and the `x_cvparse.educationLevels` truth uses its
  enum. That is why `education.level` is reported only as a cvparse-only diagnostic and is left
  out of the overall score.
- **The cvparse prompt was not tuned on this dataset** (no changes to `src/` were made from
  benchmark results), but the people who wrote it also wrote the CVs, so shared assumptions about
  what a Spanish CV looks like are likely.
- **The baselines are disadvantaged by design.** open-resume and resume-parser detect sections by
  English headings ("Experience", "Education", "Skills"); these CVs use Spanish headings
  ("EXPERIENCIA LABORAL", "FORMACIÓN", "HABILIDADES"), except two `mixed` CVs. Their low section
  scores measure exactly that gap, which is the point of the benchmark, but they say little about
  how those parsers do on the English CVs they were built for.
- **open-resume reads only PDFs.** Its DOCX and image CVs are skipped (reported as coverage, not as
  zeros); compare systems on the "PDF only" column for equal inputs.
- **resume-parser is fed cvparse's extracted text.** Its own reader needs the poppler `pdftotext`
  binary, so the harness gives it the reading-order text from cvparse's `extractText` (two-column
  ordering and DOCX text boxes included). It inherits cvparse's PDF/DOCX reading, good or bad,
  and cannot read scans (it has no OCR).
- **Synthetic and clean.** See the limitations below; real-world exports are messier.

Contributions of independently written CVs (synthetic, never a real person's) are the best fix
for these biases.

## Known limitations

- Synthetic: layouts imitate real templates but are cleaner than real-world exports (no ligatures,
  embedded fonts, broken encodings, headers/footers or photos with text).
- PDFs use the standard Helvetica font (WinAnsi), so every character is in the text layer; real
  CVs often embed subset fonts with missing ToUnicode maps.
- Scans are rendered, not photographed: rotation < 1°, sparse specks, no blur, skew or shadows.
- "verano 2020" (season) is stored with year precision (`2020`) because the season month depends on
  the hemisphere; cvparse itself normalizes it to `2020-06` by convention.
- Truth covers the scored fields only; certifications, publications, interests and references are
  not annotated.
- One page per CV in most layouts; a few single-column CVs flow onto a second page.
- Spanish only (plus two mixed CVs); no Portuguese or English CVs.
